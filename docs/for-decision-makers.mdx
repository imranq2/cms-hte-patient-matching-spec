This page summarizes the specification for readers who do not need the technical detail. It adds no requirements. **The specification text governs**, and each point links to where it comes from.

## The problem it addresses

The specification's Executive Summary cites a 2020 eHealth Initiative and NextGate survey in which about 38% of U.S. providers experienced an adverse event in the prior two years that was attributable to incorrect patient matching. It says matching rates between organizations are low, at around 50%, and that some ecosystem pledgees report closer to 40% in real data exchange and worse for patient-initiated access.

The [Risk Framework](/docs/risk-framework) argues that failing to return a record that should have been returned is a real harm that goes unnoticed, because "no one logs a match that should have happened but didn't". It sets that against the risk of returning a record to the wrong person, which is discrete, detectable, and correctable.

## What it requires

In the specification's words: if the identity attributes in a query correspond to a field combination listed in Table 2, and the query produces a unique candidate subject, the responding entity SHALL return matching records for that patient within the permitted scope for that query context. The Purpose and Core Principle 3 add that this is subject to the administrative controls in section IV.D and Table 4. <strong>Failing to return records when a query satisfies a Table 2-listed combination and produces a unique candidate is non-compliant.</strong>

- **Table 2** lists approved combinations of identity attributes (for example, first name, date of birth, and phone number). Each is listed with a conservative probability of collision at or below 2e-12, with the exception of two relationship-based rows whose caveats the specification flags as unresolved.
- <strong>A unique candidate is required.</strong> A match is satisfied only when it produces a unique candidate at the source system. When there is more than one candidate, the specification describes escalation and, in some cases, declining.
- <strong>Data is cleaned first.</strong> Implementations normalize identity attributes and remove placeholder values such as test names or default dates of birth.
- <strong>Verified identity for patient-facing apps.</strong> A patient-facing app SHALL include an IAL2 Claims Token issued by a Credential Service Provider (section III.D). Core Principle 12 says covered entities may request records from other covered entities without a signed token, and that patient-facing apps not covered by HIPAA may only request records by transmitting one.

See [Summary and Purpose](/docs/summary-and-purpose) and [Core Principles](/docs/core-principles).

## Who it applies to

The Executive Summary says CMS intends to publish the proposal as part of the CMS Interoperability Framework, which ecosystem pledgees agree to follow. Because Medicare is also a respondent in the Payer pledge category, CMS will be a direct implementer as well as the sponsor. Compliance is assessed at the source-system level for each participant (Core Principle 4).

## Two ways to comply

The specification describes two compliance paths:

- <strong>Path A, Assumed Compliance (rules-based).</strong> The audit sections describe Path A audits as verifying the correct application and integrity of the Table 2 rules.
- <strong>Path B, Empirical Compliance (performance-based).</strong> The audit sections describe Path B audits as focused on macro-level outcome metrics such as match, no-candidate, and ambiguous rates, correction rate, and appeal rate.

It also proposes (it is not yet in effect) a phased adoption period and a documented safe harbor for organizations whose current matching performance is demonstrably at or above its collision and recall benchmarks, whatever method they use. The paths are named in the Executive Summary and described in [Audit and Monitoring](/docs/audit-and-monitoring). The safe harbor is in [Implementation and Testing](/docs/implementation-and-testing), and the validation requirements are in [Validation Requirements](/docs/validation-requirements).

## What it deliberately does not do

- It does not set governance or protocol recommendations beyond matching, such as what data is returned, exchange purposes, or enforcement (Purpose).
- It does not govern wire-transaction payload formats. It governs source-system matching at responding endpoints (Purpose).
- It does not permit full SSN or full ITIN in matching queries. Using the last four digits is optional, and choosing not to collect SSN or ITIN is not non-compliance ([SSN and ITIN](/docs/ssn-and-itin)). The IAL2 token field table lists a full-SSN claim as optional, which the specification does not reconcile with this; see [Status](/docs/status).

## Timing

The specification is a draft open for public comment. Its effective date has not been set. See [Adoption Timeline](/docs/adoption-timeline) and [Status](/docs/status).

## What organizations typically need to plan for

These are the activities the specification describes, not additional requirements:

- Normalization and placeholder handling for every identity field ([Normalization](/docs/normalization)).
- Evidence of performance on the ONC patient matching test dataset, or a successor or approved third-party assessment, including precision, recall, and false-positive rate ([Validation Requirements](/docs/validation-requirements)).
- Audit and monitoring sufficient to explain match decisions and detect degradation ([Audit and Monitoring](/docs/audit-and-monitoring)).
- The specification says it is maintained through a documented governance process ([Governance and Versioning](/docs/governance-and-versioning)).
